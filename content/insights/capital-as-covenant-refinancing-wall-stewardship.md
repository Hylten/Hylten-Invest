The refinancing wall is a stewardship event before it is a financial event. A concentrated block of mid-market maturities lands between 2026 and 2029, into a banking system that will not refinance at prior terms. Owners who built capital under one moral order now face a test of how they hold it.

Scripture frames this exact moment. "A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children, but the sinner's wealth is laid up for the righteous."

The inheritance survives when the steward understands what the asset is for. This briefing examines the wall, the capital response, and the covenant logic that separates durable ownership from forced transfer.

## The Scheduled Test

The maturities are public record. Loans written in the 2021-2023 vintage cluster in 2026-2029, carrying leverage multiples and covenant-lite terms that banks now price as risk. The rollover gap is structural, produced by regulation, not by demand.

For the Christian family office, the wall is not a market event to be hedged. It is a stewardship test with a published schedule. The owner who prepares has options; the owner who waits accepts terms set by whoever holds liquidity.

Preparation is itself a moral act. It acknowledges that the asset belongs to the next generation, not to the current convenience. The families that treat the wall as a calendar of stewardship decisions preserve the inheritance.

## Bank Retreat and the Covenant Alternative

Bank retrenchment follows capital-requirement arithmetic, not sentiment. Basel risk-weighting makes mid-market leveraged exposure expensive, and compliance costs compound the penalty. The retreat shows in commitment letters: lower amounts, tighter covenants, shorter tenors.

The alternative is covenant capital. Private credit advances against collateral, not policy, and structures facilities around the asset's true purpose. The lender and the steward align because both measure the same thing: the durable value of the operating asset.

This alignment is the covenant logic of capital. The facility is not an instrument of extraction; it is a structure of accountability. The borrower answers for the asset, and the lender answers for the terms.

## The Asset Under the Covenant

Asset-based facilities price what exists. Receivables, inventory, equipment, and real assets carry the advance. The borrower monetizes balance-sheet value that banks ignore, and the structure rewards operational discipline.

The discipline matters because stewardship is operational before it is financial. A business run with order, clean books, and maintained assets qualifies for covenant capital. A business run for extraction does not.

The Gnosjö tradition teaches the sequence: work first, accumulate second, preserve third. The families that lived this sequence enter the wall with balance sheets that covenant lenders recognize. The families that skipped it face the wall alone.

## The Operational Discipline

The covenant response is operational before it is financial. A business that runs with order, clean books, and maintained assets qualifies for covenant capital at favorable terms. A business that runs for extraction does not.

The discipline shows in the balance sheet. Receivables are documented, inventory is managed, equipment is maintained, and the operating margin is verifiable. The asset-based lender advances against exactly this evidence.

The Gnosjö tradition teaches the sequence: work first, accumulate second, preserve third. The families that lived this sequence enter the wall with balance sheets that covenant lenders recognize. The families that skipped it face the wall alone.

## The Generational Transfer

The wall accelerates transfer decisions. Owners facing a rollover gap choose between refinancing at new terms or selling into a market of prepared buyers. The choice is not merely financial; it is a decision about what the family is for.

Christian family offices hold a specific advantage in this moment. Their capital has a mandate: preserve, grow, and transfer in order. The mandate filters which refinancings to accept, which sales to resist, and which buyers to trust.

The families that sequence character before capital outperform every plan that sequences capital first. The wall rewards preparation, and preparation is a character trait before it is a strategy.

## The Wall as Commission

The refinancing wall is not a punishment. It is a commission to execute stewardship at scale. The families that meet it with covenant capital, operational discipline, and generational clarity emerge with the inheritance intact.

The market will reprice assets, but the covenant does not reprice. The mandate remains: build the heir, harden the asset, transfer the trust. The families that keep the sequence keep the inheritance.

## Summary

The 2026-2029 refinancing wall tests how families hold what they built. Bank retreat removes the easy path, and covenant capital provides the structured alternative. The response is operational before it is financial: clean books, maintained assets, disciplined ownership.

The stewardship sequence decides the outcome. Work first, accumulate second, preserve third, transfer in order. The families that execute the sequence through the wall carry the inheritance to the next generation, and the inheritance carries the mandate with it.
