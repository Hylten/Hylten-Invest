Debt is not neutral. Scripture treats it as a relationship with obligations, limits, and a horizon. The Jubilee laws in Leviticus 25 built release into the economic order: debts were forgiven every fifty years, land returned to its original family, and the slave economy reset. The principle is that no obligation outlives its purpose and no family loses its inheritance forever.

The modern capital market inverted that logic. Covenant-lite credit removes the limits, extends the horizon, and treats the borrower's future as collateral. The family office that borrows on those terms does not violate a rule. It violates a structure, and the structure collects its due.

## The Stewardship of Borrowing

Stewardship begins with ownership. The earth is the Lord's, and everything in it, and the steward manages what belongs to another. Borrowing does not change the ownership question. It adds a creditor to the relationship and makes the creditor a co-claimant on the steward's faithfulness.

The biblical text is explicit about the posture. Proverbs 22:7 states that the borrower is servant to the lender. The family office that refuses to understand this sentence misunderstands its own balance sheet. Leverage is servitude with terms, and the terms decide whether the servitude builds or breaks.

The wise steward borrows only what the asset can repay without the asset being sold. The covenant structure protects both sides: the lender receives a promise backed by discipline, and the borrower receives capital without surrendering the future. The covenant, not the interest rate, is the real price of credit.

## Jubilee as Capital Structure

The Jubilee was not charity. It was a scheduled reset that prevented wealth from concentrating permanently and prevented families from being permanently dispossessed. Leviticus 25:10 commands liberty throughout the land and the return of every man to his possession.

Translated into capital structure, the Jubilee principle requires three disciplines. First, the term of the obligation must be finite and matched to the asset's life. Second, the security must be real and identifiable, never the borrower's entire future. Third, the structure must include a release mechanism that returns the asset to the family when the obligation is satisfied.

The family office that applies these disciplines builds a balance sheet that survives generations. The family office that borrows without them builds a debt trap with a compound interest engine.

## The Covenant-Lite Problem

Covenant-lite credit is the anti-Jubilee. It removes the financial covenants that would trigger early conversation, extends maturities beyond the asset's useful life, and prices the risk into the margin instead of the structure. The borrower gets flexibility, and the flexibility becomes the mechanism of destruction.

The evidence is visible in the current cycle. Private credit expanded rapidly through the last decade, and the structures carried fewer protections than the bank debt they replaced. Per the BlackRock 2026 Private Markets Outlook, asset-based financing is the segment where a profound increase in opportunities is expected, and the same report notes the expansion acclimated more borrowers to private credit.

The stewardship question is not whether the capital is available. The question is whether the structure honors the family's future. Covenant-lite credit answers no, because it removes the very mechanisms that force discipline.

## The Covenant Structure That Serves

A covenant-based facility for a family enterprise carries identifiable security, finite terms, and honest covenants. The lender holds a first lien on defined assets, the amortization matches the asset's cash generation, and the financial covenants create early warning, not punishment.

The structure requires the family to report. Monthly borrowing-base certificates, audited receivables, and equipment registers are not bureaucratic friction. They are the discipline that keeps the steward honest with the owner and the creditor. The family that cannot produce the reports cannot safely carry the debt.

The covenant also protects the next generation. A facility that amortizes and releases assets on schedule hands the successor a clean balance sheet. A facility that rolls and capitalizes interest hands the successor a claim on the inheritance.

## The Generational Window

The wealth transfer now underway makes the question urgent. Fortune reports that the transfer of up to 124 trillion dollars from Baby Boomers to younger generations is projected, roughly 1 300 000 miljarder SEK, and Bank of America's Private Bank study finds the share of inherited businesses among wealthy Americans reaching 23 percent in 2026.

The National Christian Foundation's research adds the stewardship dimension: only 28 percent of Christian Millennials who will inherit wealth say their family has a plan for the transfer, and that the wealth has already begun to pass to the next generation.

The family office that borrows without a covenant transfers a liability. The family office that structures debt on Jubilee principles transfers a discipline. The difference shows up in the third generation, where the first family still owns the enterprise and the second family still owns the debt.

## The Stewardship Standard

The standard is not avoidance. Scripture does not forbid borrowing, and Proverbs 22:7 describes a reality rather than a prohibition. The standard is structure: finite terms, real security, honest reporting, and a release mechanism.

"The good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children, but the sinner's wealth is laid up for the righteous."

    Proverbs 13:22

The inheritance in view is not only the asset. It is the character that manages the asset. Capital structure is character made visible, and the covenant is the tool that carries both across generations. The family office that treats debt as covenant builds on the Jubilee. The family office that treats debt as convenience builds on sand.

## Summary

Debt carries a covenant or it carries a curse. The Jubilee discipline requires finite terms, real security, honest reporting, and a scheduled release. Covenant-lite credit removes those protections and transfers the cost to the next generation. The family office that structures leverage as stewardship builds an inheritance that survives, and the family that borrows without structure inherits the debt.
