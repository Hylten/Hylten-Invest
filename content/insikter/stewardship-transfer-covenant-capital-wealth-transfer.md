## The Transfer Window Demands a Capital Structure That Honors the Covenant

The largest intergenerational wealth transfer in history is underway. An estimated 85 trillion SEK in enterprise value will change hands across the Nordics and North America by 2030, according to Cerulli Associates 2025. First-generation founders aged 60 to 75 control this capital. The question is not whether it transfers. The question is whether the capital structure preserves what the founders built: the values, the governance, the stewardship mandate.

Bank credit fails this test. Bank lending is transactional. It prices risk through covenants that protect the lender, not the borrower's legacy. When the founder exits, the bank's covenant package offers no mechanism to ensure the next generation honors the founding values. The capital is indifferent to the covenant.

Covenant capital is different. It embeds the stewardship mandate into the capital structure itself. The lender's return is tied to the borrower's fidelity to the founding governance. The capital serves the covenant, not the reverse.

"The borrower is servant to the lender" (Proverbs 22:7). But the lender who structures capital as covenant becomes a partner in the stewardship mandate, not a claimant on the assets.

## The Capital Structure Gap

Traditional bank financing operates on a simple premise: collateral coverage and cash-flow coverage. The covenants protect the lender's downside. They do not protect the borrower's governance. When the founder exits, the bank's covenants remain static while the governance vacuum opens. The new leadership inherits the debt but not the covenant discipline that made the business viable.

Covenant capital inverts this. The capital structure includes governance covenants: board composition requirements, succession plan enforcement, values-alignment reporting, stewardship metrics. The lender's return is linked to governance fidelity. When governance drifts, the cost of capital adjusts. The capital structure becomes a guardian of the founding mandate.

Cerulli Associates 2025 reports that 78 percent of family office principals cite "governance continuity" as the primary concern in the wealth transfer, ahead of tax efficiency (62 percent) and asset allocation (45 percent). The market is signaling the gap. Capital that ignores governance continuity is capital that fails the stewardship test.

## Asset Hardening as Covenant Enforcement

Asset hardening is the operational expression of covenant capital. It transforms operating assets into financial instruments that carry the stewardship mandate forward. The hardened asset generates predictable cash flows. Predictable cash flows support covenant compliance. Covenant compliance preserves the governance structure. The governance structure preserves the founding values.

The mechanism is structural:

- Receivables are hardened through documented collection processes that survive ownership transfer
- Inventory is hardened through documented liquidation values that survive management change
- Equipment is hardened through maintenance records that survive technician turnover
- Real estate is hardened through title clarity that survives generational transfer

Each hardened asset class carries a covenant: the processes that generate its value must be maintained. The capital that finances the hardening enforces the covenant. The lender monitors the hardening metrics. The borrower maintains them. The covenant holds.

Deloitte Family Succession 2026 reports that families who implement asset-hardening structures before the transfer achieve 40 percent higher governance continuity scores and 25 percent lower transition costs. The hardening is not financial engineering. It is stewardship engineering.

## The Bank Credit Failure Mode

Bank credit fails the stewardship test in three structural ways:

First, bank covenants are financial, not governance. They measure debt service coverage, leverage ratios, current ratios. They do not measure board independence, succession readiness, values alignment, or stewardship fidelity. When the founder exits, the financial covenants may hold while the governance collapses. The bank is repaid. The legacy is lost.

Second, bank lending is procyclical. When the business faces transition stress (the exact moment when governance is most vulnerable), the bank tightens terms. Advance rates drop. Pricing widens. Covenants tighten. The capital withdraws when the stewardship mandate needs it most.

Third, bank credit has no mechanism for values transmission. The loan agreement contains no clause for "founder's values," "stewardship fidelity," or "generational continuity." The capital is indifferent to the covenant. Indifferent capital cannot serve a stewardship mandate.

## Covenant Capital as Infrastructure

Covenant capital builds the governance covenant into the capital structure:

- **Governance covenants**: Board composition must include independent stewardship directors. Succession plan must be documented, tested, and updated annually. Values alignment must be reported quarterly.
- **Stewardship metrics**: Employee tenure, customer retention, supplier continuity, community investment. These metrics are reported to the lender. They are the early-warning system for governance drift.
- **Succession enforcement**: The capital structure includes triggers for succession plan activation. If the founder exits without documented succession, the capital structure adjusts: pricing widens, advance rates drop, governance oversight increases.
- **Values alignment reporting**: The borrower reports on stewardship fidelity: employee engagement with founding values, customer retention through transition, supplier relationships maintained, community commitments honored.

The lender who provides covenant capital becomes a stewardship partner. The lender's return is tied to governance fidelity. The lender monitors the stewardship metrics. The borrower maintains them. The covenant holds.

"The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance, but everyone who is hasty comes only to poverty" (Proverbs 21:5). Covenant capital rewards diligence. Bank credit cannot serve a stewardship mandate.

## The Stewardship Transfer Protocol

The transfer protocol requires four structural elements:

**1. Capital formation with governance mandate**: The capital formation workstream includes stewardship mandate alignment as a filter. LPs who share the stewardship values commit directly. The capital formation CRM tracks mandate alignment as a primary filter.

**2. Origination with stewardship screening**: The origination engine screens for stewardship alignment. Targets with documented succession plans, governance packages, and values alignment score higher. The origination signal includes stewardship metrics.

**3. Asset hardening with covenant enforcement**: The special situations desk structures asset-backed facilities with governance covenants. Hardened assets carry governance covenants. The collateral audit includes governance audit.

**4. Stewardship reporting and enforcement**: Quarterly stewardship reporting to the lender. Annual governance audit. Succession plan testing. Values alignment verification. The lender enforces the covenant through pricing and advance rate adjustments.

The four elements share a single data layer. The capital formation workstream feeds the origination workstream. The origination workstream feeds the special situations workstream. The stewardship reporting feeds back to all three. The flywheel is mechanical. It does not depend on market sentiment.
